Teacher notes

Aim: distinguish laws from rules set by places or groups. Designed for Entry 3 ESOL adults, individually, in pairs or on a classroom screen.

  1. Read the two definitions together.
  2. Ask learners to explain their choice before tapping an answer. Do not judge only by the place: a law can apply inside a shop, workplace or college.
  3. Read the feedback and discuss: “Who sets this?” and “What could happen if someone breaks it?”
  4. Use “Practise missed questions” to revisit mistakes.

Language: “must” means it is necessary; “must not” means it is not allowed; “fine” means money paid as a punishment; “minimum wage” means the lowest hourly pay allowed for an eligible worker; “nationality” means the country a person belongs to as a citizen; “policy” means a set of rules; “shift” means a period of work.

Important distinction: laws are rules too. Here, “rule” means an organisational rule rather than a law. The same behaviour can break both. A rule is not always optional or unimportant. A college rule is not automatically a law. The rules in this game are examples, not statements of Bolton College policy.

Context: UK everyday situations, using the law in England for this activity. Legal details can differ across UK nations. These are simplified learning examples. Not every legal problem is a police matter: discrimination may be dealt with by a court or employment tribunal, and minimum pay can be enforced by HMRC.
